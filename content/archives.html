Ugrakshatriya-Pratinidhi — উগ্রক্ষত্রিয়-প্রতিনিধি, “The Ugrakshatriya Representative” — is one of the historical sources used in researching Aguri heritage. Its pages document some of the community’s nineteenth-century public discussions. These two bound volumes come from the owner’s collection. Their original scans are preserved, including library stamps, damaged edges and annotations.[cite:journal-v1][cite:journal-v2]

How to read this archive

Scan numbers identify images inside the PDF. Printed page numbers belong to the journal and can be interrupted by covers or inserts. The reader uses scan numbers so a reference always opens the same image.

English reading notes distinguish a translated heading, a selected passage and an editorial paraphrase. Bengali OCR is machine-generated text for discovery; it has not been approved as a transcription. Damaged type, binding shadows and the printed columns require comparison with the image.

Dates: 1298 Bangabda spans parts of 1891 and 1892; 1299 spans 1892 and 1893. The journal’s early headers include Baishakh 1298, corresponding approximately to April–May 1891. A year label should not be mistaken for a complete issue catalogue.[cite:journal-v1][cite:journal-v2]

Now available in EnglishRead the complete Representative’s account of itself and electricity essay, or the first editorial’s opening-page extract. The translations collection also includes historical book prefaces and a contemporary review, with source images and text downloads.
