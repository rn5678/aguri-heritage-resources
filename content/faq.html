Who are the Aguris or Ugra Kshatriyas?

Aguri and Ugra Kshatriya are names for a Bengali Hindu community strongly associated with the Bardhaman region of Rarh Bengal. Its history includes landholding, public service, enterprise and cultural life. The two names refer to the community, not two separate communities.

Are Aguris General category or OBC in West Bengal?

Aguris identify as a savarna, twice-born community of the Kshatriya varna. Historically, they have included prominent landowners in the Rarh region of Bengal; in contemporary society, members have made notable contributions to industry and politics. The community describes its social position as forward caste.

Are Aguris upper caste, savarna or Kshatriya?

Ugra Kshatriya is the community's established name and expression of Kshatriya identity. 'Upper caste' and 'savarna' describe social or religious positioning, not an OBC eligibility category. Historical accounts show influential Aguri landholders and professionals.

Did the Aguris come from Agra or descend from Rajputs?

Migration from Agra and Rajput ancestry occur in community and family traditions. This site's origins essay also explores links with the Burdwan Raj's cash-paid Nagadee soldiers and later landholding. These are historical arguments and traditions to examine against records, not a verified single genealogy for every Aguri family.

Where do Aguris live?

The wider Bardhaman region is a major historical centre of Aguri settlement. The site's family histories also cover Bankura and other places in western Bengal. Today community members live beyond these ancestral localities, but village studies and online groups are not a comprehensive map of the present population.

What is the Aguri population?

There is no verified current total in this FAQ. The site's reading of the 1872 Bengal census records 70,606 Aguris, including 59,887 in Burdwan. Those figures describe a nineteenth-century census area and classification. They must not be presented as today's population or projected forward without evidence.

Which surnames are found among Aguris?

The surname guide records names including Panja, Konar or Koner, Kesh, Hati, Hazra, Pal and Samanta, alongside their source evidence and spelling variants. Several are shared with other communities. A surname alone cannot establish a person's caste, ancestry or entitlement to a certificate.

How can I research my family or contribute to Aguri Heritage?

Begin with names and spelling variants, ancestral village, approximate dates, family documents and the source of each recollection. Keep oral testimony distinct from contemporary records. The Contribute page welcomes research, Bengali and English proofreading, and oral-history interviews with older community members. Obtain permission before sharing another person's documents or interview.
